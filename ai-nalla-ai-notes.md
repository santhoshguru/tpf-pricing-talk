# Stop picking a winner. Start assigning jobs.

Speaker notes for the AI Nalla AI deck (`ai-nalla-ai.html`). The slides are speaker prompts; the words live here. Same notes are embedded in the deck and shown in the presenter window (press P in the deck).

Speaker: Santhosh Guru (santhoshguru.com)
Host: Ravi (AI Nalla AI cohort, session 9)
Length: 60 minutes. 45 minutes talk including one live demo, 15 minutes Q&A.

The one-line frame: Nalla samayal does not come from hiring the most famous cook. It comes from a good kitchen, a clear note, a recipe you trust, and someone tasting before it hits the leaf. Nalla AI is the same.

The one honest gap in the analogy, said aloud on slide 2: a real cook remembers regulars. This one does not. Nothing you say changes the cook.

## Slide 1. The wrong question (3 min)

Headline: "Which AI tool should I use?" is the wrong question.

- Open with the question everyone has asked. Say it is reasonable, and say it is exactly why the toolkit feels baffling.
- The claim: the value is not in any one chatbot. It is in the combination. A cook, a kitchen that gives him hands, your notes as his memory, and you tasting everything.
- The AI Nalla AI frame: nalla samayal does not come from hiring the most famous cook. It comes from a good kitchen, a clear note, a recipe you trust, and someone tasting before it hits the leaf. Nalla AI is the same.
- Tell them what they leave with: nine words explained through one kitchen, a way to decide which job goes to which tool, one dish to cook this week, and an honest story about the time it went wrong.
- Set expectation: mostly show and tell. Four short kitchen slides, then real systems.

## Slide 2. The cook, his training, his last kitchen, and the torn token (5 min)

Headline: The cook knows every cuisine. He has never been inside your house. And he forgets you the moment the token is torn.

- "Imagine a cook who grew up in a kitchen in Kumbakonam and has cooked everywhere since. Sambar, biryani, payasam, Chettinad, Udupi, name it. Enormously skilled. Has never been inside your house."
- Training years (pretraining): "How did he get that skilled? Thirty years of watching, tasting and cooking everything. The AI did the same with most of the internet. It knows food. It does not know your amma's sambar."
- Last kitchen (post-training): "Two cooks, same thirty years. One spent a decade in a Udupi hotel, one in a Chettinad mess. Same vegetables, different food. Same knowledge, different house style. Different AI apps feel different for the same reason. Different last kitchen."
- One token (inference): "Put him at a mess counter. You buy a token, hand it over, he cooks, you eat, token torn. Tomorrow he has no idea who you are. Every conversation with AI is one token. Nothing carries over unless you carry it."
- The honest gap, said plainly: "A real cook remembers regulars. This one does not. Nothing you say changes the cook. That is the single most important thing to know about AI."
- Bridge: "So if he is brilliant, forgetful, and knows nothing about you, what decides what he can actually do for you? Now bring him from the mess to your house."

## Slide 3. The kitchen decides what the cook can do (3 min)

Headline: Same cook. Different kitchen. Different food.

- "Put him in your home kitchen with one gas stove and a mixie, he feeds six. Put him in a kalyana mandapam kitchen with wood fire, big andaas, ten helpers and a Swiggy tablet, he feeds five hundred and takes orders. The cook did not change. The kitchen did."
- Anchor to what they have used: "When you set up a ChatGPT Project or a Custom GPT, you were arranging a kitchen. Codex is a kitchen with a grinder for one specific job. NotebookLM is a kitchen built around your own pantry. Different kitchens decide what the cook can touch."
- "The word engineers use for the kitchen is harness. You will not hear it again tonight. I will just say kitchen."
- Land the point: choosing an AI tool is mostly choosing a kitchen. The cooks are closer to each other than the advertising says. The kitchen decides what gets done.

## Slide 4. The note at the door and the notebook page (3 min)

Headline: The note at the door is about your house. The notebook page is about the dish.

- "He arrives at your house for the function. You hand him a page: twelve people, amma is diabetic, no onion today, thatha wants extra karam, serve by six. Without that page he cooks for a stranger." Then: "You already learnt this in an earlier session. Good. The new part is next."
- "Every house has that notebook. Sambar the way we make it, drumstick, less tamarind, kadugu at the end. You wrote it once. Now any cook who walks in makes our sambar. That is a skill. A saved instruction for one dish you keep making."
- The distinction in one breath: the note is about your house. The notebook page is about the dish. You want both saved somewhere the kitchen can reach, so you stop rewriting them.
- Do not explain where the pages are stored or how they sync. If asked: "Plain text files in a folder the kitchen can read. The deep dive on that is a separate session."

## Slide 5. One cook, one stove, one dish. The wedding kitchen. (3 min)

Headline: Nobody asks the payasam master to fry vadai.

- Stack the definitions: a cook, in a kitchen, with your note and one notebook page, doing one dish. Nothing new was added. Slides 2 to 4 were combined.
- Why one dish each: a cook with one job is easier to trust and easier to taste-check. Same for AI agents. This is the mix-and-match idea in the title. Different stations can use different cooks.
- "My setup is a wedding kitchen where some of the cooks are AI." Thirty-second aside: "My first team had no business purpose at all. It stitched my reading history, my film log and my book highlights into one taste library. Zero stakes, pure joy. That is where I learnt the kitchen bends to the house, not the other way round."
- Bridge: "Now the same kitchen with real stakes."

## Slide 6. The serious proof: an eight-course sappadu, tasted between every course (8 min)

Headline: Eight stations in a row. Taste after every one. And some shelves in the pantry stay locked.

- This is the main case. Ravi flagged it as the most relatable. Give it the most time.
- Walk the eight phases quickly. For each, say what comes out of that station and where you tasted before the next one started. Show one real artefact if possible: one tailored pack and the one-page resume it produced.
- Spend real time on the pantry shelves. This is the part the audience has not heard and it answers the "is it safe to give AI my stuff" worry directly. "The open shelf anyone can use. The locked shelf I open for the cook. And paati's secret podi I grind myself and never hand to any cook. Decide up front what the AI never sees, and make it structurally impossible, not something you remember to be careful about."
- The numbers (181 packs, 136 resumes) prove that reuse compounds. The first took hours. The latest takes minutes because the pantry already holds everything.
- The takeaway is two principles, not the tooling: hand over the note and the notebook, and never skip tasting. Say those two sentences and stop.

## Slide 7. Live demo: the wedding kitchen, live (11 min, mostly screen share)

Headline: These are cooks you delegate to, not a counter you stand and wait at.

- Switch to screen share. Slide is a placeholder for the agenda and for recovery if the demo stalls.
- Step 1 (3 min). Show the workspace. Open one or two real conversations, including the one where the agents helped plan tonight's session. "It looks like a team chat where some teammates are AI. It is a wedding kitchen where some cooks are AI."
- Step 2 (5 min). Build one skill live. Paste a pre-written notebook page (do not type from scratch; it burns time and adds risk). Attach it to one agent. Show before and after: same request without the page, then with it. "Same cook. Our sambar now, not his."
- Step 3 (2 min). Place one real order with an agent that takes a few minutes. Say aloud: "I am not going to stand at the counter. We will come back before Q&A." Switch back to slides. The place-the-order-and-walk-away moment is the lesson.
- Optional (2 min, only if ahead): show how AI search bots read santhoshguru.com live.
- Fallback: recorded dry run in a second tab. If anything fails, switch within ten seconds and narrate over the recording. Do not debug live.
- Skip entirely: how the agent is built, how files sync, anything GitHub. Agreed with Ravi. If asked, one sentence and offer the deep-dive session.

## Slide 8. What broke: the substitute cook (4 min)

Headline: The regular cook went native for Pongal. The substitute followed the same notebook. The sambar was still different.

- [SANTHOSH: story specifics] Tell the actual story briefly: which model was swapped, which job it was doing, what changed in the output, how the review step caught it, and the decision to roll back.
- The lesson is not "that cook was bad". It is that a cook's last kitchen (slide 2) shows up in real work even when the notebook is identical, and the only reason it did no damage is that someone tasted.
- Second lesson: it is tempting to stop tasting once the last hundred dishes were fine. Do not. Reliability is what makes you stop looking, and that is exactly when a substitute lands.
- This slide earns trust for everything before it. Do not skip it to save time. Cut the optional website aside on slide 7 instead.

## Slide 9. Start tonight: one dish (4 min)

Headline: One dish. One note. One kitchen. One taste. That is the whole talk in miniature.

1. Pick one dish you make every week (one recurring chore).
2. Write one page about your house (your note at the door).
3. Hand it to the one kitchen already on your phone (a ChatGPT Project or Custom GPT counts).
4. Taste. Fix it. Note what you fixed.
5. Write what worked into the notebook. Reuse it next week.

- Everything here can be done on a phone tonight with free or cheap tools. Say so.
- Point back: step 3 is the kitchen. Step 2 is the note. Step 5 is the notebook page. Doing it for one dish, week after week, is one cook with one job. "You have just hired your first cook."
- Before Q&A, switch to screen share and check the order placed in slide 7. Show the result. If not done, show progress and say that is also normal.

## Slide 10. Q&A (15 min)

Headline: Stop picking a winner. Start assigning jobs.

- Leave the labelled kitchen on screen for the whole Q&A. It is the recap.
- Likely questions, one-line answers:
  - "Which tool should I start with?" The kitchen already on your phone. The tool matters less than the one page you hand it.
  - "Is it safe to give AI my personal data?" Decide which shelf stays locked. Slide 6.
  - "How do I build a team like yours?" That is a deep-dive session. Tonight was the mental model. Offer to run it with Ravi if there is interest.
  - "Does this cost a lot?" The one-dish recipe is free. My wedding kitchen costs more, and I will say roughly what, because it replaced hours of work.
