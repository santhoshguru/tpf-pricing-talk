1 of 6 · Model

A model is the cook. Skilled at everything. Knows nothing about your house. Forgets you after every meal.

The samayal master. Thirty years in every kitchen. Never once inside yours.

Pretraining: his training years. Read every cookbook, cooked everywhere. For AI, most of the internet. Knows food, not your family.
Post-training: his last kitchen. Udupi hotel or Chettinad mess, same vegetables, different food. Why ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini feel different.
Inference: one token at the mess counter, torn after the meal. Nothing carries over unless you carry it.
  • "Imagine a cook who grew up in a kitchen in Kumbakonam and has cooked everywhere since. Sambar, biryani, payasam, Chettinad, Udupi, name it. Enormously skilled. Has never been inside your house." That cook is the model.
  • Pretraining: "How did he get that skilled? Thirty years of watching, tasting and cooking everything. The AI did the same with most of the internet. It knows food. It does not know your amma's sambar."
  • Post-training: "Two cooks, same thirty years. One spent a decade in a Udupi hotel, one in a Chettinad mess. Same vegetables, different food. Same knowledge, different house style. Different AI apps feel different for the same reason. Different last kitchen."
  • Inference: "Put him at a mess counter. You buy a token, hand it over, he cooks, you eat, token torn. Tomorrow he has no idea who you are. Every conversation with AI is one token. Nothing carries over unless you carry it."
  • The honest gap, said plainly: "A real cook remembers regulars. This one does not. Nothing you say changes the model. That is the single most important thing to know about AI."
  • Bridge: "So if he is brilliant, forgetful, and knows nothing about you, what decides what he can actually do for you? Now bring him from the mess to your house."
2 of 6 · Harness

A harness is the kitchen around the model. It decides what the model can actually do.

Same cook. Different kitchen. Different food.

The model thinks, writes, reasons. That is all it does on its own.
The harness gives it hands: your files, the web, tools, memory, the ability to actually do things.
Every AI app you have used is a harness. ChatGPT app, Codex, AI Studio, NotebookLM. Same model, different kitchen, different result.
  • "Put him in your home kitchen with one gas stove and a mixie, he feeds six. Put him in a kalyana mandapam kitchen with wood fire, big andaas, ten helpers and a Swiggy tablet, he feeds five hundred and takes orders. The cook did not change. The kitchen did." The kitchen is the harness.
  • Anchor to what they have used: "When you set up a ChatGPT Project or a Custom GPT, you were arranging a kitchen. Codex is a kitchen with a grinder for one specific job. NotebookLM is a kitchen built around your own pantry. Different harnesses decide what the model can touch."
  • "Harness is the engineers' word. Tonight I will mostly say kitchen."
  • Land the point: choosing an AI tool is mostly choosing a harness. The models are closer to each other than the advertising says. The harness decides what gets done.
3 of 6 · Context

Context is the note you hand over every single time. Who you are, what today needs.

The note at the door: 12 people, amma diabetic, no onion today, thatha wants extra karam, serve by 6.

Every time, because the model does not remember. Without the note he cooks for a stranger.
You already know this one. Save it once, somewhere the harness can read, so you stop rewriting it.
Decide up front what the model never sees. Paati's podi stays off the note. Make it impossible, not something you remember to be careful about.
  • "He arrives at your house for the function. You hand him a page: twelve people, amma is diabetic, no onion today, thatha wants extra karam, serve by six. Without that page he cooks for a stranger." That page is context.
  • "You already learnt this in an earlier session. Good. Tonight the point is: save it once so you stop retyping it. A ChatGPT Project's instructions, a Custom GPT's setup, a file the harness can read. That is where the note lives."
  • The pantry shelves: "The open shelf anyone can use. The locked shelf I open for the cook when I say so. And paati's secret podi I grind myself and never hand to any cook. Decide up front what the AI never sees, and make it structurally impossible, not something you remember to be careful about." This answers the "is it safe to give AI my stuff" worry.
  • Do not explain where the files are stored or how they sync. If asked: "Plain text files in a folder the kitchen can read. The deep dive on that is a separate session."
4 of 6 · Skill

A skill is one saved instruction for one job you keep doing. Written once. Any model follows it.

The notebook page: our sambar, drumstick, less tamarind, kadugu at the end.

Context is about your house. A skill is about the dish.
Write it once. Any cook who walks in makes our sambar, not his.
This is the new idea tonight. Stop re-explaining the same job in every chat. Save the recipe.
  • "Every house has that notebook. Sambar the way we make it, drumstick, less tamarind, kadugu at the end. You wrote it once. Now any cook who walks in makes our sambar. That is a skill. A saved instruction for one dish you keep making."
  • The distinction in one breath: context is about your house. A skill is about the dish. You want both saved somewhere the harness can reach, so you stop rewriting them.
  • Examples they can picture: the way you want a weekly report written, the format of your customer replies, how you like a lesson plan structured. Each one is one page. Each one is a skill.
  • Do not explain storage or syncing. If asked: "Plain text files in a folder the kitchen can read. Separate session."
5 of 6 · Agent

An agent is a model, in a harness, with your context and one skill, doing one job.

One cook, one stove, one notebook page, one dish. Nobody asks the payasam master to fry vadai.

Nothing new was added. The four words so far, combined, pointed at one job.
One job each, because an agent with one job is easier to trust and easier to check. Several agents with different jobs is a team. Different jobs can use different models.
You stand at the pass. Delegate, then check.
  • Stack the definitions: a model, in a harness, with your context and one skill, doing one job. Nothing new was added. The last four slides were combined.
  • Why one job each: an agent with one job is easier to trust and easier to check. This is the mix-and-match idea in the title. Different stations can use different models. Stop picking a winner, start assigning jobs.
  • "My setup is a wedding kitchen where some of the cooks are AI." Thirty-second aside: "My first team had no business purpose at all. It stitched my reading history, my film log and my book highlights into one taste library. Zero stakes, pure joy. That is where I learnt the kitchen bends to the house, not the other way round."
  • Bridge: "The sixth word is you."
6 of 6 · You

You are the last step. Check the output before it goes out. Every time.

Tasting before it hits the leaf. The regular cook went home for Pongal. The substitute followed the same notebook page. The sambar was still different.

Same context, same skill, different model, different result. The last kitchen shows up in real work.
Caught at tasting, not on the leaf. Nobody outside ever saw it.
Context and skills protect you a lot. Checking protects you completely. Reliability is exactly when you are tempted to stop looking.
  • This is the sixth word, and it is you. The note and the notebook are about setting the kitchen up. Tasting is what you do every time food comes out.
  • SANTHOSH: story specifics. Tell the actual story briefly: which model was swapped, which job it was doing, what changed in the output, how the review step caught it, and the decision to roll back.
  • The lesson is not "that cook was bad". It is that a model's last kitchen (slide 2) shows up in real work even when the skill is identical, and the only reason it did no damage is that someone checked.
  • Second lesson: it is tempting to stop checking once the last hundred outputs were fine. Do not. Reliability is what makes you stop looking, and that is exactly when a substitute lands.

Start tonight

One job. One page of context. One app. One check.

One dish. One note. One kitchen. One taste. The whole talk in miniature.

1 Pick one job you do every week. One recurring chore.
2 Write one page of context about yourself. The note at the door.
3 Hand it to the app already on your phone. A ChatGPT Project or Custom GPT counts. That is your harness.
4 Check the output. Fix it. Note what you fixed.
5 Save what worked as a skill. Reuse it next week.
  • Everything here can be done on a phone tonight with free or cheap tools. Say so.
  • Point back: step 3 is the harness. Step 2 is context. Step 5 is a skill. Step 4 is you. Doing it for one job, week after week, is one agent with one job. "You have just hired your first cook."
  • Before Q&A, switch to screen share and check the order placed in the demo. Show the result. If not done, show progress and say that is also normal.
